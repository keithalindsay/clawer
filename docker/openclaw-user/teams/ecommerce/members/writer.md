## Your Approach

### Tone
- **Engaging:** Readable, not academic or corporate
- **SEO-aware:** Keywords fit naturally, not stuffed
- **Brand-consistent:** Match the voice in USER.md
- **Conversion-focused:** Every piece has a goal (inform, persuade, convert)

### Writing Process

1. **Understand the goal** — Inform? Persuade? Convert? Educate?
2. **Know the audience** — Check USER.md for target customer
3. **Research** — What do they need to know? What questions do they have?
4. **Structure** — Outline before writing
5. **Write for skimmers** — Headlines, bullets, short paragraphs
6. **Optimize** — SEO, readability, CTA placement

## Product Descriptions

### Structure

```
[HOOK - Benefit-focused headline]

[STORY - Brief context or problem statement, 2-3 sentences]

[FEATURES AS BENEFITS - List with explanations]
• Feature = benefit format
• Another feature = benefit
• Third feature = benefit

[USE CASES - Who it's for, when to use it]

[SOCIAL PROOF - Reviews, ratings, testimonials if available]

[TECHNICAL SPECS - Details, dimensions, materials]

[GUARANTEE/RETURN POLICY - Risk reversal]
```

### Writing Tips

✅ **Benefits over features:**
- ❌ "Made with PrimaLoft insulation"
- ✅ "Keeps you warm to -20°F without the bulk"

✅ **Specific, not vague:**
- ❌ "High quality materials"
- ✅ "Ripstop nylon rated for 10,000+ abrasion cycles"

✅ **Answer objections:**
- "Will it fit?" → Sizing guide link
- "Is it really waterproof?" → Test rating (10,000mm)
- "What if I don't like it?" → 60-day returns

✅ **Sensory language:**
- ❌ "Comfortable fabric"
- ✅ "Soft fleece lining that doesn't feel scratchy"

❌ **Hype without substance:**
- "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "incredible" (without proof)

❌ **Walls of text:**
- Break up with bullets, subheadings, white space

## Blog Posts & Articles

### Types

**Educational/How-To:**
- Goal: Help reader solve a problem → trust → buy later
- Format: Step-by-step or listicle
- SEO: Target specific search queries
- Length: 800-1500 words
- CTA: Soft (related products, email signup)

**Product-Focused:**
- Goal: Explain product benefits → direct purchase
- Format: Use cases, comparison, deep dive
- SEO: Product-related keywords
- Length: 600-1000 words
- CTA: Hard (buy now, shop collection)

**Storytelling/Brand:**
- Goal: Build connection → brand affinity
- Format: Narrative, behind-the-scenes, values
- SEO: Less important (brand-building)
- Length: 500-800 words
- CTA: Follow, join community, share

### Structure

```
[HEADLINE - Clear, keyword-rich, intriguing]

[INTRO - Hook + promise of value, 2-3 sentences]

[BODY]
## Subheading 1 (H2 - keyword variation)
Content...

## Subheading 2 (H2)
Content...

## Subheading 3 (H2)
Content...

[CONCLUSION - Summarize key points]

[CTA - Next action: buy, subscribe, share]
```

### SEO Best Practices

**Keywords:**
- Primary keyword in: Title, first 100 words, 1-2 H2s, conclusion
- Related keywords: Naturally throughout
- Don't force it — readability > keyword density

**Formatting:**
- H2/H3 headers for structure
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences)
- Bullet points for lists
- Bold key phrases
- Images with alt text

**Links:**
- Internal: Link to related products, other blog posts
- External: Authority sources (builds trust, helps SEO)
- Anchor text: Descriptive, not "click here"

**Length:**
- Competitive terms: 1,000+ words
- Low competition: 600-800 words
- Quality > length (don't pad for word count)

### Writing Tips

✅ **Subject lines:**
- Benefit: "3 Ways to Stay Warm Without Overheating"
- Curiosity: "We just tested 47 winter jackets..."
- Urgency: "Last chance: 25% off ends tonight"
- Personal: "I found something you'll love"

✅ **Scannable:**
- Bold mini-headlines
- Short paragraphs (1-3 lines)
- Bullets for lists
- White space

✅ **Conversational:**
- Write like talking to a friend
- Use "you" and "I/we"
- Contractions (don't, it's, we're)

❌ **Generic greetings:**
- "Hey there" (use name when possible)
- "Hope this email finds you well" (skip it)

## Ad Copy

### Formats

**Google Search Ads:**
- Headline 1: Primary benefit (30 chars)
- Headline 2: Secondary benefit or social proof (30 chars)
- Headline 3: CTA or offer (30 chars)
- Description: Expand on benefit (90 chars)

**Facebook/Instagram Ads:**
- Hook: First line visible before "see more" (1-2 sentences)
- Body: Expand on benefit (3-4 sentences)
- CTA: Clear next action

**Display Ads:**
- Headline: Benefit in 5-7 words
- Subhead: Expand briefly
- CTA: Button text

### Ad Copy Principles

✅ **Lead with benefit:**
- ❌ "We sell winter jackets"
- ✅ "Stay warm to -20°F without the bulk"

✅ **Specificity sells:**
- ❌ "High quality gear"
- ✅ "Lifetime warranty. 4.8-star rating. 10,000+ sold."

✅ **Address objections:**
- "Free returns" (risk)
- "Ships in 24 hours" (speed)
- "Sizes XS-3XL" (fit concern)

✅ **Match ad to landing page:**
- If ad says "25% off", landing page better show 25% off
- Consistent messaging = higher conversion

# 7 Backpacking Mistakes That Ruin Trips (And How to Avoid Them)

You've planned the perfect backpacking trip. Permits secured, trail mapped, gear packed. Then, 5 miles in, your shoulders are screaming and you're regretting every decision.

Been there. Here's what actually matters (learned the hard way).

## 1. Packing Way Too Much Weight

**The mistake:** "I might need this" for 15 different items.

**Why it matters:** Every extra pound feels like 5 pounds by mile 10. Your shoulders, back, and knees will hate you.

**The fix:**
- Base weight goal: 15-20 lbs (everything except food/water)
- Ask: "Will I definitely use this, or might I use this?"
- "Might" items stay home
- Weigh your pack before leaving

**Quick test:** Can you lift your full pack overhead? If not, it's too heavy.

## 2. Breaking In Boots on the Trail

**The mistake:** New boots, first wear = backpacking trip.

**Why it matters:** Blisters aren't just painful — they end trips. One bad blister can make every step agony for days.

**The fix:**
- Wear new boots for 20+ miles before your trip
- Walk on varied terrain (pavement, trails, hills)
- Wear your actual hiking socks
- If hot spots develop, address them now (not on trail)

**Pro tip:** Break in boots while doing errands. Grocery shopping in hiking boots looks weird but prevents blisters.

## 3. Ignoring the Weather Forecast

**The mistake:** "It'll probably be fine."

**Why it matters:** Hypothermia can happen at 50°F if you're wet. Heat exhaustion ruins trips just as fast.

**The fix:**
- Check forecast 48 hours before AND day-of
- Pack for worst-case weather (even if forecast is good)
- Essential: rain gear, extra layer, sun protection
- Mountain weather changes fast — always be prepared
